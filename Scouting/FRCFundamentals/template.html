Intro to FRC Scouting

What is Scouting?

Scouting in FIRST Robotics Competition (FRC) is the systematic process of observing, recording, and analyzing robot and team performance during a competition. The ultimate goal is to collect enough reliable data to make informed strategic decisions — especially during alliance selection and playoff matches.

Every team on the field has strengths, weaknesses, and tendencies. A well-run scouting operation turns those observations into structured data, and that data into competitive advantage.

Pro Tip

Even a basic spreadsheet with consistent data is more valuable than a sophisticated system with inconsistent inputs. Start simple, stay consistent.

Types of Scouting

There are two primary scouting modes used in FRC competitions, each serving a different purpose:

Pit Scouting

Conducted in the pit area before or between matches. Scouts visit each team's pit to gather information about robot capabilities, autonomous routines, and reliability history. This gives you qualitative context that match data alone cannot provide.

Match Scouting

The bread and butter of any scouting operation. One scout is assigned to track one robot per match, recording specific quantitative metrics such as scoring actions, cycle times, and end-game results.

Common Mistake

Tracking too many metrics per match leads to missed entries and fatigued scouts. Limit your form to the 5–8 metrics that actually influence alliance selection.

Example: Simple Match Form

Below is a minimal example of a match scouting data structure in JSON format — the same data a digital scouting app might produce after each match:

JSON 
{
  "match": 14,
  "team": 4321,
  "scout": "Alex M.",
  "auto": {
    "mobility": true,
    "scoredHigh": 2,
    "scoredLow": 0
  },
  "teleop": {
    "cycles": 8,
    "avgCycleTime": 22.4
  },
  "endgame": "climbed",
  "defense": false,
  "notes": "Very consistent auto, struggled with defense late game"
}
